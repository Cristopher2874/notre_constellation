# Centrale Nantes / Lyon / Lille — School and Lab Analysis (Context Bank)

Purpose of this document: this is a reference file, not application prose. It exists to hold the verified facts about professors, labs, industry links, and student clubs at the three target schools, so future work threads (drafting the speech, the projet professionnel, the projet académique, interview prep) can pull from one consistent, checked source instead of re-deriving it each time. It is deliberately kept factual and source-noted rather than persuasive.

Methodology, unchanged from the prior version: every entry is checked against a lab page, an official faculty bio, an institutional partner listing, or an independent competition/results archive, not inferred from a course description. Where something could not be verified to that standard it is flagged explicitly as a gap rather than presented with the same confidence as a confirmed item. Source material is paraphrased throughout, never quoted.

Note on this revision: this replaces the earlier version of the file. The earlier version carried a partial, redundant draft of the Lyon/Lille sections ahead of the full document; that duplication has been removed. The content itself has also been reweighted, not just cleaned up — see the reading frame below.

READING FRAME — CURRENT POSITION (read this before using any table below)

The target role is systems integrator, not device designer and not industrial-automation engineer. The working self-description is: an engineer who connects sensing and embedded control intelligence to a physical mechanism that assists human movement. The mechanism itself — its kinematics, its actuator choice, its structural design — is explicitly not the intended contribution; that is mechanism-design work, better suited to a specialized track, and claiming it as a personal research goal is a risk, not an asset.

The application domain is human movement assistance (disability, reduced mobility, physical therapy-adjacent use cases), not industrial manufacturing or factory-floor cobotics. Industrial-controls material found at any of the three schools is retained in this document, but only as evidence that a given lab or team is broad rather than narrowly clinical — it is background breadth, not the destination.

The demonstrator is a single arm/hand joint, chosen for portability and demonstrability (something that can be described in one sentence and potentially shown at an interview), not because hand/arm anatomy is the specific interest. Any professor or lab entry below that focuses on hand- or arm-specific mechanism work should be read as ecosystem evidence that this field is active at that school, not as a template to imitate or a scope the project is bound to.

The admissions constraint that governs how every entry should be weighted: the target program selects for ingénieur généraliste profiles — broad training across mechanics, fluid mechanics, programming, physics, and control, with specialization chosen later from a wide menu. A school or lab entry that reads as narrowly biomedical-device-specific works against this profile. The clearest worked example of the wrong model is INSA Lyon (see the dedicated section below) — a specialized-track school whose medical-robotics research is exactly the kind of narrow anchor to avoid presenting as a personal ambition. Centrale Nantes' Robotics specialization officially names "ingénieur intégrateur" as one of its two exit profiles; that phrase is the anchor for staying generalist while still being specific about the assistance-robotics field.

## 2. The INSA distinction (read this before writing anything comparing Lyon's medical robotics to Nantes)

This is a cross-cutting note, not a Lyon-specific one, because it defines the trap the whole project is trying to avoid.

Ampère (UMR CNRS 5005) is a joint laboratory associated with Université Claude Bernard Lyon 1, École Centrale de Lyon, INSA Lyon, and CNRS. Its medical-robotics activity — upper-limb exoskeletons for human motor-control study, surgical simulators (BirthSIM, laparoscopy training), teleoperation and haptics for medico-surgical gesture — is carried out by researchers formally attached to INSA Lyon, not to École Centrale de Lyon. The two names verified doing this work are Richard Moreau and Tanneguy Redarce, both INSA Lyon faculty within Ampère's "Méthodes pour l'ingénierie des systèmes" department. Source: Ampère's own institutional pages and the GDR Robotique national lab directory, both attributing the medical-robotics/surgical-gesture line to these two INSA-affiliated names specifically.

The practical use of this fact is not to disqualify Lyon — it is to make explicit, on the record, what the specialized-track alternative to a Centrale application would look like, so that no future document accidentally borrows Ampère's medical-robotics framing as if it were a Centrale Lyon strength. Korniienko's robust-control work, by contrast, is directly on the École Centrale de Lyon faculty and is genuinely generalist (complex-systems and MEMS control, not organ- or limb-specific), which is exactly why it remains usable.

## 6. Arguments this unlocks, under the current generalist / assistance-robotics scope

1. Nantes now has a direct, current, named topical anchor that the other two schools don't have. Chablat and Aoustin, and the 2024 published hand-prosthesis mechanism work behind them, are a materially stronger and more specific claim than anything available at Lyon or Lille for this particular field. This should be the lead argument for Nantes as first choice, ahead of the earlier Ghanes/Renault framing.

2. All three schools remain genuinely generalist at the level that matters for admission. None of the three verified secondary/primary anchors — Chablat and Aoustin's assistance-robotics work, Korniienko's complex-systems control, El Najjar's navigation-safety estimation — requires or implies a biomedical-device specialization. The one place a narrow biomedical-device specialization does exist in this research (Ampère's exoskeleton and surgical-simulator work) belongs to INSA Lyon, not to any of the three target schools, which is itself a usable talking point: it shows the difference between the school being applied to and its more specialized neighbor is understood, not overlooked.

3. The ROMAS team's own breadth at Nantes — industrial cobotics, medical cobotics, and assistive-device work under one team — is an argument in its own right for why a generalist engineer should train there: the host environment itself doesn't force a choice between industrial and medical framing, so neither does the candidate.

4. Two named institutes, not just two labs, still holds as a structural argument: IRT Jules Verne (Nantes) and IRT Railenium (Lille) are real, named research institutes with a formal Centrale academic partnership. Lyon's equivalents (Groupe SEB, Renault Trucks) are single companies near campus, not shared research institutes — a real asymmetry worth naming plainly if asked to compare the three ecosystems.

5. Three confirmed clubs (pending the Lyon naming check) mean the team-robotics thread — the FRC-style dynamic — survives any school choice: Nantrobot, APROB/ERACL, and A.R.L.V. all compete in the same national circuit.
